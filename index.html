Self-directed research

Cultural Spillover: does pop culture predict brand demand?

A self-directed study testing whether rising search interest in the manga/anime Nana predicts consumer interest in Vivienne Westwood, using Google Trends data, time-series regression with lagged variables, and Chanel as a control brand. Found a positive lagged association between Nana interest and Vivienne Westwood interest — observational data, so no claim to causation, but it shows the rigor of the method rather than overclaiming the result.

Self-directed research

Saving by Default: does automatic enrolment change savings behaviour?

A replication of Somville and Vandewalle's (2018) study on automatic account deposits and savings behaviour in rural India, comparing people paid into a savings account against those paid in cash. Rebuilt the analysis pipeline end to end — data cleaning, baseline balance checks, main treatment effects, and heterogeneous effects — confirming clean randomisation (0 of 23 baseline variables significant) before replicating the core treatment effect results.

Self-directed research

Paint Politics: is art ever truly independent?

A research essay in Cultural Economics tracing how political and economic conditions have shaped art movements — from Renaissance patronage in Medici Florence, to Nazi Germany's dueling 1937 exhibitions and Soviet Socialist Realism, to Dada's refusal of art itself, to how behavioural economics now treats public art as a tool of influence.
